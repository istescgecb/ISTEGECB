Information Technology Engineering

Information engineering (IE), also known as information technology engineering (ITE), information engineering methodology (IEM) or data engineering, is a software engineering approach to designing and developing information systems. A data engineer is someone who creates big data ETL pipelines, and makes it possible to take huge amounts of data and translate it into insights. They are focused on the production readiness of data and things like formats, resilience, scaling, and security. Data engineers usually hail from a software engineering background and are proficient in programming languages like Java, Python and Scala.

Job Profiles

1

Data scientist

Simply the highest paying job in the IT field with an average salary of 10 LPA. Data scientists are big data wranglers, gathering and analysing large sets of structured and unstructured data. A data scientist’s role combines computer science, statistics, and mathematics. They analyze, process, and model data then interpret the results to create actionable plans for companies and other organizations.

Skills required

  • Understand Machine Learning algorithms
  • Create data models
  • Code in language like Python, R, SAS and other analytical tools
  • Identify business issues and provide appropriate solutions
  • Master's degree in data science

2

Computer Service Technician

Referred to as computer repair technicians and they help repair computer hardware and software. Some of the common tasks are replacing defective components, removing spyware and viruses, dissembling hardware, and running diagnostic tests.

Skills required

  • Experience in assembling and repairing computers
  • CompTIA A+ certification

3

Cyber security

Protect the security of computer systems and networks. Cyber security specialists enjoy an excellent average salary.

Skills required

  • Experience is critical
  • Major certifications
  • Broad technical knowledge

4

Data Analyst

Mainly focuses on the collection, analysis and interpretation of data. It is easier to transition from data analyst to data scientist.

Skills required

  • Bachelor's degree in Information Technology
  • Bachelor's degree in Data Analysis.
  • Must be well versed with programming languages.

5

Data Centre Technician

The data centre technician is necessary to keep a company’s data safe and secure. You may come across server repair and management, monitoring system processes, maintaining equipment to prevent problems and also providing technical education and support.

Skills required

  • Skills in IT computer networking, hardware repair, troubleshooting and inventory management.
  • Expertise in technical writing.
  • Able to work on your feet for long stretches and carry heavy objects.

6

Hardware engineer

Hardware engineers oversee the manufacture and installation of computer systems, servers, circuit boards, and chips, as well as the testing of equipment. They also work with routers, printers, and keyboards.

Skills required

  • Degree in computer engineering.

7

Multimedia Developer

Multimedia developers are skilled in computer programming and visual artistry. They design software and create multimedia applications by generating and manipulating animations, graphic images, text, sound, and video.

Skills required

  • IT-graduate strong in visual arts.

8

Programmer

Programmers writes the code needed for programs to function. They also test software and update existing software. Many are employed by software companies.

Skills required

  • Should know programming languages.

9

Software Developer

Mainly involved in software creation. Professionals in software development create and build out software. They provide detailed instructions and guidelines for the programmers who write the code.

Skills required

  • Should know programming languages.

10

Systems Analyst

Systems analysts use their expertise to introduce computer systems, or to modify existing systems as a way to boost technical efficiency and business productivity.

Skills required

  • Work experience, programming knowledge and project management skills.
  • Computer science, information management systems, or other IT-related degree

11

Systems Engineer

Systems engineers design, set up and manage computer systems. They often work closely with programmers, administrators and engineers. These professionals not only develop and test but also evaluate personal computers, circuits, software, and other system elements.

Skills required

  • Computer science, information technology, or engineering degree.
  • Communication and organisation management skills.

12

Tech Support

Tech support workers give essential technical support and troubleshooting services to end-users. In-house technicians provide support exclusively for employees of the company, while remote help desk technicians provide technical support to customers.

Skills required

  • Strong understanding of software and computer hardware.
  • Excellent communication skills.

13

Web Developer

Web developers design and establish websites. They are skilled in both programming and the design of pages, navigation and user interfaces.

Skills required

  • Knowledge of search engine optimisation techniques
  • Bachelor’s degree in a relevant field

14

Network Engineer

Network engineers design and set up networks. Duties may include placing physical equipment, setting up electronic equipment needed to activate equipment, and determining the appropriate antenna to ensure the best possible coverage.

Skills required

  • Computer science degree
  • Specialised certification

15

Network Administrator

This professional manages and troubleshoots computer networks. The network administrator is responsible for organising and maintaining computer systems.

Skills required

  • Degree in an IT-related field.
  • Network-specific experience.
  • Specialised certification in network administration.

16

Full stack engineer

Software expert who’s equally proficient in client-side development and server-side development. Full Stack Developers are familiar with each layer of tech stacks that go into the making of a software product. They are highly valued by large companies and small start-ups alike.

Skills required

  • Broad skill set and extensive knowledge base.
  • Experience in software development.

17

IoT Engineer

Internet of Things refers to physical objects – vehicles, household appliances, wearables, and much more – that are connected to the Internet and can therefore transfer data online using embedded sensors. A specialist in connected objects, an IoT engineer develops innovative services that help users to receive, control and manage information.

Skills required

  • Knowledge in computer programming and network security.
  • Experience in working with front-end and back-end systems as well as custom-built firmware and hardware.
  • Knowledge of sensors, machine learning and AI.
  • Knowledge of software like Netbeast, Neura, and OpenSensors, Node.js and have expert knowledge of Linux OS.
  • Should be well versed with multiple programming languages such as Embedded-C, Embedded C++, JavaScript, and Python.

18

VR/AR developer

Virtual reality/ augmented reality developer, one who helps in creating and maintaining a virtual 3-d environment may it be for games or any other uses.

Skills required

  • Well versed in programming languages
  • General XR knowledge
  • Machine learning
  • 3-d modelling
  • Graphic rendering

19

AI engineering

Artificial intelligence engineers are responsible for developing, programming and training the complex networks of algorithms that make up AI so that they can function like a human brain.

Skills required

  • Software development
  • Programming
  • Data science
  • Data engineering

20

Help desk technician

When a computer user or employee is having problems with hardware, software, or a network, they call on a help desk specialist to assist. A help desk technician might maintain, install, or troubleshoot hardware and software, resolve networking issues, or help resolve other problems for other company employees.

Skills required

  • Knowledge of computer systems and troubleshooting.
  • Certifications, associate degrees, or bachelor’s degrees .

21

Database administrator

Database administrators store, organize, and secure data. This is an especially critical task for companies with large information systems. Ensuring that databases operate efficiently helps companies analyze and leverage this data for growth.

Skills required

  • Bachelor's degree in CS or IT

22

Site reliability Engineer

Site reliability engineers (SREs) keep the websites and apps we use up and running smoothly. SREs develop automated solutions for common development and operational tasks, like latency monitoring, capacity planning, and emergency responding.

Skills required

  • Site reliability or DevOps experience and related skills.
  • Bachelor’s degree

23

Cloud Engineer

Cloud engineers help businesses migrate applications, functions, and processes to the cloud. They typically oversee the design, configuration, and monitoring of cloud-based services like Amazon Web Services , Microsoft Azure, and the Google Cloud Platform.

Skills required

  • Knowledge of cloud platforms.
  • Bachelor’s degree, master’s degree, or equivalent experience

24

Blockchain Engineer

Blockchain engineers are responsible for developing and implementing solutions and architectures that are related to the blockchain technology. The adoption of digital payments is increasing drastically in India and therefore blockchain has appeared as a key innovator in the fintech sector especially.

Skills required

  • Proficiency in programming
  • Knowledge and understanding of Ripple, Etherium, R3, and bitcoin technologies.
  • In-depth understanding of consensus methodologies
  • Security protocol stacks and crypto libraries

25

Full stack developer

Full-stack developers are responsible for overall web development and maintenance. Their ability to work on both the front end and the back-end functionalities of any application makes them particularly significant in the tech department of any company. Reports show that there has been a 20% spike in the demand of full-stack developers in India in recent times.

Skills required

  • Bachelor’s degree in Mathematics, computer science, statistics, or related field
  • Proficiency with fundamental front end languages such as HTML, CSS and JavaScript
  • Front end and back end technology skills
  • Version control system
  • Coding skills
  • Cloud skills
  • Database skills

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) Engineer

Emerged rapidly in 2023, this role bridges the gap between massive Large Language Models (LLMs) and a company’s private data. Rather than training models from scratch, RAG engineers build pipelines that allow AI to securely search internal documents and databases in real-time to generate accurate, hallucination-free answers.

Skills required

  • Orchestration frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex)
  • Python and API integrations
  • Understanding of text embeddings and chunking strategies
  • Familiarity with LLM context window optimization

Vector Database Administrator

Traditional SQL and NoSQL databases cannot efficiently handle the high-dimensional data (embeddings) used by modern AI. Vector DBAs manage the specialized databases (like Pinecone, Milvus, or Weaviate) that store the “memories” and semantic relationships required by generative AI applications.

Skills required

  • Vector database architecture (Pinecone, Qdrant, Milvus)
  • Nearest-neighbor search algorithms (HNSW)
  • Data pipeline automation and scaling
  • High-availability distributed system management

AI Agent Developer / Agentic Architect

While prompt engineers talk to AI, Agent Developers build autonomous AI systems that talk to *each other*. Rising to prominence in 2024 and 2025, they design multi-agent workflows where AI models are given goals, tools, and the autonomy to browse the web, write code, and execute multi-step tasks without human intervention.

Skills required

  • Agentic frameworks (AutoGPT, CrewAI, Microsoft AutoGen)
  • Advanced software architecture and modular design
  • API tool-use integration for LLMs
  • State management for autonomous workflows

Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Engineer

With the formalization of NIST's quantum-resistant encryption standards in 2024, enterprises and governments began aggressively hiring PQC engineers. Their sole focus is upgrading legacy cryptographic systems (like RSA and ECC) to new lattice-based algorithms before quantum computers become powerful enough to break them.

Skills required

  • Deep understanding of applied cryptography
  • Lattice-based algorithms (Kyber, Dilithium)
  • Legacy system auditing and migration
  • C, C++, or Rust for secure cryptographic implementation

Spatial Computing Software Engineer

Spurred by the launch of Apple's VisionOS and advancements in Meta's ecosystem, this evolved from traditional VR/AR development. Spatial computing engineers build applications that seamlessly blend digital UIs with the physical world, focusing on eye-tracking, hand gestures, and persistent digital objects in physical spaces.

Skills required

  • VisionOS (SwiftUI, RealityKit) or advanced Unity/Unreal
  • Understanding of spatial mapping and LiDAR data
  • 3D math and physics programming
  • Ergonomic and gaze-based UI/UX design

AI Safety & Alignment Engineer

As AI models grew immensely powerful, companies needed engineers strictly dedicated to "Red Teaming." They actively try to break, jailbreak, or trick AI systems into generating harmful, biased, or unauthorized content, and then design mathematical guardrails (Constitutional AI) to ensure the model aligns with human ethics and corporate policies.

Skills required

  • Adversarial machine learning (Red Teaming)
  • Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)
  • Bias detection and statistical analysis
  • Knowledge of global AI legislation (e.g., EU AI Act)

AI Hardware Accelerator / NPU Engineer

With CPUs and GPUs bottlenecking AI inference, the industry shifted heavily toward custom silicon (Neural Processing Units / NPUs). These engineers specialize in optimizing software and machine learning models to run efficiently on this highly specialized hardware, vital for bringing AI to local laptops and mobile devices.

Skills required

  • Hardware-software co-design
  • Model quantization and pruning techniques
  • C++, CUDA, and low-level memory management
  • Understanding of Edge AI constraints (battery, thermal limits)

Platform Engineer

Evolving past traditional DevOps, Platform Engineers treat internal developer tools as a product. They build automated, self-service internal developer platforms (IDPs) that allow software engineers to deploy code and provision cloud infrastructure without needing to understand the underlying complex server configurations.

Skills required

  • Kubernetes and container orchestration
  • Infrastructure as Code (Terraform, Pulumi)
  • Go or Python programming
  • Product management mindset for internal tools

FinOps (Cloud Financial Operations) Engineer

As cloud computing bills exploded between 2022 and 2026, companies needed engineers dedicated to cloud cost optimization. FinOps engineers analyze cloud usage, optimize server allocations, purchase reserved instances, and rewrite inefficient code to drastically reduce monthly AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud invoices.

Skills required

  • Deep knowledge of cloud billing and pricing models
  • Data analysis and visualization tools
  • Scripting for automated resource scaling
  • Communication skills to align finance and engineering teams

Synthetic Data Engineer

Because real world data is often restricted by privacy laws or lacks diversity, these engineers use AI to generate massive, statistically accurate datasets of fake information. This synthetic data is then used to safely train other machine learning models without violating user privacy.

Skills required

  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and diffusion models
  • Data privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA)
  • Advanced statistical analysis
  • Python and data manipulation libraries (Pandas, PySpark)

Zero Trust Security Architect

The shift to remote work permanently killed the idea of a secure corporate network perimeter. Zero Trust Architects design IT environments where no device, user, or application is trusted by default, regardless of their physical location or network connection.

Skills required

  • Identity and Access Management (IAM)
  • Micro-segmentation of networks
  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) protocols
  • Endpoint detection and response (EDR)

LLM Optimization Engineer

Running massive AI models is expensive and slow. Optimization engineers focus on techniques like quantization, pruning, and knowledge distillation to shrink large language models so they can run efficiently on smaller servers, laptops, or even mobile phones without losing their smarts.

Skills required

  • Low-level hardware programming (CUDA)
  • Model weight quantization (converting 16-bit to 4-bit)
  • PyTorch and TensorFlow compilation
  • Understanding of neural network architecture

AI Threat Analyst

A new branch of cybersecurity focused entirely on protecting AI systems. They defend against prompt injection attacks, where malicious users try to trick an AI into revealing secret instructions, and data poisoning, where hackers corrupt the data used to train the AI.

Skills required

  • Adversarial machine learning
  • Penetration testing for AI APIs
  • Understanding of prompt engineering vulnerabilities
  • Cybersecurity fundamentals

Data Mesh Architect

Moving away from giant, centralized data lakes, Data Mesh Architects build decentralized data architectures. They treat data as a product, setting up infrastructure that allows individual departments (like HR or Sales) to own and serve their own data to the rest of the company securely.

Skills required

  • Distributed systems architecture
  • Data governance and access control
  • Cloud data warehousing (Snowflake, BigQuery)
  • Domain-driven design

Smart Contract Security Auditor

With billions of dollars locked in decentralized finance (DeFi), these highly specialized software engineers review blockchain code line by line. They look for vulnerabilities, logic flaws, and potential exploits in smart contracts before they are deployed to the blockchain.

Skills required

  • Solidity or Rust programming
  • Deep knowledge of Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM)
  • Cryptography and blockchain architecture
  • Reverse engineering

Decentralized Identity Developer

Instead of logging into websites with Google or Facebook, these developers build systems where users own their own digital identity using blockchain and cryptographic proofs. They focus on privacy-preserving login methods and digital credentials.

Skills required

  • Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs)
  • Web3 protocols and wallets
  • Public key infrastructure (PKI)
  • Standards like W3C Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs)

Algorithmic Bias Auditor

Also known as AI Ethicists, these professionals analyze AI systems for unfair bias regarding race, gender, or age. They inspect training datasets and test model outputs to ensure automated decisions in hiring, lending, or healthcare are legally compliant and socially responsible.

Skills required

  • Data science and statistical fairness metrics
  • Understanding of sociology and ethics
  • Python libraries for explainable AI (SHAP, LIME)
  • Compliance and regulatory knowledge

WebAssembly (Wasm) Developer

WebAssembly revolutionized browser capabilities by allowing high-performance languages like C++ and Rust to run natively on the web. These developers build heavy applications like video editors, 3D games, and complex enterprise software that run directly inside a web browser at near-native speeds.

Skills required

  • Rust, C++, or Go programming
  • JavaScript and browser APIs
  • Memory management and performance profiling
  • WebAssembly toolchains

Developer Experience (DX) Engineer

A specialized frontend and tooling role focused entirely on making other developers happier and more productive. They improve internal documentation, speed up local build times, streamline Git workflows, and create custom IDE plugins for their company's engineering teams.

Skills required

  • Advanced scripting (Bash, Node.js, Python)
  • CI/CD pipeline optimization
  • Technical writing and documentation
  • Empathy for user experience

Low-Code / No-Code Architect

As programming became more democratized, this role emerged to manage enterprise platforms like PowerApps, Bubble, or OutSystems. They design the secure backend architecture and data models that allow non-technical business users to drag and drop their own internal software applications securely.

Skills required

  • Expertise in enterprise low-code platforms
  • API integration and webhooks
  • Database design and management
  • Business process mapping

Data Quality Engineer

Machine learning models are only as good as the data fed into them. Data Quality Engineers build automated pipelines to clean messy data, remove duplicates, fix formatting errors, and monitor data streams in real-time to catch anomalies before they ruin an AI's training run.

Skills required

  • SQL and data modeling
  • Python and data testing frameworks (Great Expectations)
  • ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) pipelines
  • Analytical problem solving

Edge Security Architect

With computing moving to the edge (smart devices, local servers), the security perimeter expanded massively. These architects design security protocols specifically for IoT devices, 5G towers, and localized computing nodes that are physically vulnerable and outside traditional data centers.

Skills required

  • IoT and embedded systems security
  • Hardware encryption and trusted execution environments
  • Network security at the edge
  • Lightweight cryptography

Quantum Algorithm Developer

While still a bleeding-edge field, tech giants began hiring these developers to write software for early quantum computers. They translate complex classical problems (like chemical simulation or logistics routing) into quantum circuits using specialized programming languages.

Skills required

  • Quantum physics and linear algebra
  • Quantum programming languages (Qiskit, Q#)
  • Algorithm design and optimization
  • Python and C++

AI Product Manager

Building an AI product requires entirely different workflows than traditional software. AI Product Managers understand the limitations, costs, and ethical risks of machine learning models. They bridge the gap between data scientists, software engineers, and business executives to launch AI features successfully.

Skills required

  • Basic understanding of machine learning concepts
  • Agile product management
  • Data-driven decision making
  • UX design for AI interactions

Spatial Data Pipeline Engineer

Powering augmented reality requires processing massive amounts of 3D data in real-time. These engineers build the backend pipelines that ingest LiDAR scans, photogrammetry data, and live video feeds, processing them quickly enough to stream 3D environments to headsets and mobile devices.

Skills required

  • 3D graphics formats and rendering engines
  • High-performance computing (C++, Rust)
  • Cloud streaming architectures
  • Computer vision basics

Open Source Program Manager

As modern IT became entirely reliant on open-source code, large enterprises created dedicated roles to manage this relationship. They ensure the company complies with open-source licenses, manage corporate contributions to external projects, and build internal cultures of collaborative coding.

Skills required

  • Deep knowledge of software licensing (GPL, MIT, Apache)
  • Community building and developer relations
  • Software supply chain security
  • Project management and technical communication

Applied AI Researcher

Unlike academic researchers who publish papers, Applied AI Researchers take the latest breakthroughs from academic labs and figure out how to implement them into commercial products. They focus on turning theoretical models into fast, stable, and profitable software features.

Skills required

  • PhD or strong background in computer science / mathematics
  • Expertise in deep learning frameworks
  • Software engineering best practices
  • Ability to read and implement complex research papers
