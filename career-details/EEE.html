Electrical and Electronics Engineering

Electrical engineering is an engineering discipline concerned with the study, design and application of equipment, devices and systems which use electricity, electronics, and electromagnetism. It emerged as an identifiable occupation in the latter half of the 19th century after commercialization of the electric telegraph, the telephone, and electrical power generation, distribution and use. Electrical engineering is now divided into a wide range of different fields, including computer engineering, systems engineering, power engineering, telecommunications, radio-frequency engineering, signal processing, instrumentation, photovoltaic cells, electronics, and optics and photonics. Many of these disciplines overlap with other engineering branches, spanning a huge number of specializations including hardware engineering, power electronics, electromagnetics and waves, microwave engineering, nanotechnology, electrochemistry, renewable energies, mechatronics/control, and electrical materials science.

Job Profiles

1

Electrical engineer

When you decide to become an electrical engineer, you're committing yourself to a profession that involves developing, designing, testing and supervising the manufacturing of electrical devices and equipment, including navigation systems, electric motors and power generation equipment. Average starting salary: Rs. 23,500 /-

Skills required

  • Problem-solving skills
  • Basic circuit knowledge
  • Communication skills
  • Programming skills
  • Numerical skills

2

Electronics engineer

Electronics engineers create, design and develop everyday devices such as mobile phones, portable music devices and computers. Electronic engineering offers the chance to produce new innovations and developments in telecommunications, robotics, computing hardware, and power and electrical equipment.There is plenty of scope to specialise within the field, with areas of expertise including audio, visual and light electronic equipment; control systems and automation; and microelectronics (computer chips) and telecommunications. There is currently a shortage of electronics engineers around the world – a situation likely to continue for some time to come.

3

Electrical designer

Electrical designers are in charge of creating electrical schematics, layouts, and other grid plans for their organization. They must develop effective, safe electrical diagrams, provide assistance in the installation of equipment and systems, and perform design computations using their engineering skills.

4

Test engineer

Test Engineers inspect and report on the quality of products through the entire production cycle. Depending on the product, they may use different criteria to check quality, such as functionality, durability, safety and speed. The role of Test Engineers is to thoroughly check materials, procedures and mechanical or electrical systems to ensure that customers get high-quality, functional products. They run tests on various components and features in order to identify and fix technical issues. Experienced Test Engineers might also be responsible for training and managing junior team members.

Skills required

  • Familiarity with various testing techniques
  • Hands-on experience with Quality Management Systems (QMS)
  • Knowledge of quality standards and regulations
  • Great troubleshooting skills
  • Analytical mindset

5

Electronic design automation engineer:

Research, design, develop, or test electronic components and systems for commercial, industrial, military, or scientific use employing knowledge of electronic theory and materials properties. Design electronic circuits and components for use in fields such as telecommunications, aerospace guidance and propulsion control, acoustics, or instruments and controls.

6

Systems analyst

Systems analysts analyse how well software, hardware and the wider IT system fit the business needs of their employer or of a client. They write requirements for new systems and may also help implement them and monitor their effectiveness

Skills required

  • Strong analytical skills
  • Attention to detail
  • Teamwork and Interpersonal skills
  • Written and verbal communication skills
  • Flexibility, Adaptability and Initiative

7

Manufacturing systems engineer

Manufacturing systems engineers design, plan, develop and install systems including manufacturing equipment and assembly lines. This covers all aspects of manufacturing processes. They may also be involved in the design and building of a manufacturing plant

8

Micro Electronics Engineer

Microelectronics is a subdivision of electronics that focuses on the design of small electronic parts like semiconductors, circuit boards, and microchips that are used in the design of new biomedical, electronic, aerospace, and information technologies devices and systems. Microelectronic engineers construct prototypes of new designs, perform specialized tests, and record and interpret data on the performance of new models

9

Electrical Engineering Technician

An electrical engineering technician takes an active role in the development of projects designed by electrical engineers and computer hardware engineers. They set up and test a prototype and working electronics components, such as navigation systems; they also report on the electronics' performance, and make suggestions for change as needed. People employed in this job usually have an associate's degree in electrical engineering technology. Industry certification in electrical power testing is available. According to the BLS, the number of jobs for electrical and electronics engineering technicians is expected to grow by 2% from 2019-2029. These professionals earned median pay of $65,260 in 2019; the BLS also reports that the states where the greatest number of electrical and electronics engineering technicians worked were California, Texas, Virginia, New York and Florida.

10

Telecommunications Engineer

Telecom Engineers are responsible for examining new network technologies and offering suggestions regarding integration. They have to ensure network availability and provide necessary maintenance and support. They are expert in managing data such as voice, calls, text, and video, over different modes of communication. They install and design different telecommunication equipment. They work in a vast field of instruments from radio and television to Satellite, computer networks, the internet, and optical fiber.

Skills required

  • Good knowledge of logging and database tables
  • Experience with designing and implementation of LAN, WAN, Wireless LAN, and WWAN
  • Fair technical understanding of network hardware, Internet standards, and protocols.

11

Electrical Design Engineer

An electrical design engineer develops new electrical systems for various applications. They are responsible for developing system specifications and layouts. The research systems ideas and draw up plans for these systems, usually using a computer program. Electrical design engineers also test their systems and make adjustments as needed. They also provide support throughout the manufacturing of the electrical systems they have designed.

Skills required

  • Strong communication
  • Analytical and problem-solving skills
  • Ability to work under pressure in a specific timeline
  • Organizational
  • Leadership
  • Creative skills

12

Instrumentation Engineer

An instrumentation engineer typically works for industries with automated processes with the sole goal of improving productivity, safety, reliability, stability, and optimization. The focus area of their work is designing, developing, installing, managing equipment that is used to monitor and control machinery. Instrumentation engineers are commonly responsible for integrating the sensors with the recorders, displays, transmitters, or control systems. They are responsible for the testing and maintenance of the system. They generally design or specify installation, wiring, and signal conditioning.

Skills required

  • Ability to think out of the box
  • Translate project needs into design development of hardware suitable for the task
  • Sound problem-solving skills
  • Communication skills

13

Electricians

Electricians install, maintain, and repair electrical power, communications, lighting, and control systems in homes, businesses, and factories.

14

Electrical and Electronics Installers and Repairers

Electrical and electronics installers and repairers install or repair a variety of electrical equipment in telecommunications, transportation, utilities, and their industries.

The main duties of energy engineers involve creating, testing, securing and implementing elements of an electrical plant, such as switchgear, transformers, motors, cables, generators, etc.Monitoring the overall energy consumption and find inconsistencies,Visiting project sites, to evaluate energy usage data, and to conduct audits, To Collaborate with construction managers and other engineers and provide feedback related to power systems, electric grids, backup generators, etc.Average salary in the US: 71,200 USD/year.

16

Automotive Engineer

The main duties of electrical engineers working in the automotive industry is to Work on the development of components like power units, integrated navigation systems, lighting, air conditioning, sound, new technologies used to create autonomous vehicles or fully electric cars, and to Evaluate the distribution of electric cables and power generators and decide if they meet safety regulations .Average salary in the US: 78,800 USD/year

17

Aerospace Engineer

Main duties of electrical engineers working in the aerospace industry include working on guidance and communication systems, radars or satellites , To Contribute to the development of new hybrid or fully electric propulsion systems for aeroplanes or other aircraft and to Collaborate with other aerospace engineers and specialists to determine which materials should be used to ensure the safety of the passengers and the reliability of the entire electrical system

18

Oil and Gas Engineer

The main duties of electrical engineers working in the oil and gas industries to Design, test, and improve specialised equipment (generators, electric motors) used on oil platforms and drilling rigs, To Evaluate and decide if the equipment meets the safety and reliability standards, and To Ensure the equipment can still function under challenging work conditions, such as extremely high or low temperatures. Average salary in the US: 66,360 USD/year

19

Controls engineer

Primary duties of a Controls engineers is to work and manufacture energy plants to ensure consistent, efficient and high-quality production processes. They design the plant's overall manufacturing process, making adjustments to optimize output. They improve productivity by adjusting the layout of the production floor and automating essential workflow processes. Controls engineers determine operational standards and schedule maintenance, quality and inspection reviews to align employee actions with company policy.National average salary: $60,711 per year

20

Sustainability engineer

Sustainability engineers are responsible for adjusting and creating designs to have a low environmental impact. They determine how to use renewable energy sources to accomplish a goal and find ways to limit the amount of energy waste and hazardous byproducts that could impact the environment. Sustainability engineers predict the impact of a project on the local and global ecosystem, then make recommendations on how to recycle materials, save energy and eliminate pollution.National average salary: $94,752 per year

21

Director of technology

A company's director of technology is in charge of supervising and coordinating all of the organization's technology needs. This includes planning technological infrastructure, ordering equipment and hiring employees that have advanced technical knowledge. The director of technology creates guidelines for information security, internal technology updates and data processing. They determine how to invest company funds in technological initiatives and seek out suppliers or business partners for new projectsNational average salary: $127,468 per year

Quantum Control Electronics Engineer

Quantum computers require operating environments near absolute zero. These engineers design the highly specialized microwave control electronics and cryogenic wiring systems that translate classical computer code into the signals that manipulate delicate quantum bits (qubits) without causing them to lose their quantum state.

Skills required

  • Microwave and RF engineering
  • Cryogenic electronics design
  • Superconducting circuits
  • Quantum physics fundamentals

Neuromorphic Hardware Designer

Moving away from traditional von Neumann architecture, neuromorphic engineers design silicon chips that mimic the human brain's neural structure. These "spiking neural networks" process AI tasks with a fraction of the power required by standard GPUs, making them critical for the future of mobile AI.

Skills required

  • Non-volatile memory integration (Memristors)
  • Analog and mixed-signal IC design
  • Computational neuroscience
  • VLSI layout and verification

Open RAN Telecommunications Engineer

As 5G and early 6G networks expand, telecom providers are moving away from proprietary hardware to Open Radio Access Networks (O-RAN). These engineers design and integrate interoperable, software-defined radio hardware that allows network operators to mix and match equipment from different vendors.

Skills required

  • Software Defined Radio (SDR)
  • O-RAN alliance standards
  • Cloud-native telecom architecture
  • Network virtualization (NFV)

Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) Integration Engineer

With electric vehicles becoming mainstream, V2G engineers design the bi-directional charging hardware that allows car batteries to push power back into the public grid during peak hours. They solve complex power inversion, grid synchronization, and battery degradation challenges.

Skills required

  • High-power electronics and inverters
  • Grid synchronization protocols
  • Smart meter communication standards
  • Battery lifecycle management

Solid-State Battery Electronics Engineer

Solid-state batteries promise safer and denser energy storage for EVs and consumer electronics. These engineers design the highly sensitive internal sensors and specialized Battery Management Systems (BMS) required to monitor the unique thermal and chemical behavior of solid-state cells.

Skills required

  • Electrochemistry fundamentals
  • Advanced sensor integration
  • Control systems algorithms
  • Thermal dynamics management

Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) Hardware Engineer

Working at the absolute cutting edge of biomedical electronics, BCI engineers design the micro-electrode arrays, signal amplifiers, and wireless transmission hardware that allow human brains to interface directly with computers, prosthetics, and digital environments.

Skills required

  • Biocompatible materials engineering
  • Ultra-low noise signal amplification
  • Neural signal processing
  • Micro-fabrication techniques

3D IC Packaging Engineer

Moore's Law is slowing down, so the industry is now stacking chips vertically. 3D IC Packaging Engineers design the Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs) and micro-bumps that connect multiple vertically stacked silicon dies, managing extreme heat density and microscopic electrical routing.

Skills required

  • Semiconductor physics
  • Thermal dissipation modeling
  • Advanced materials science
  • High-speed interconnect design

Smart Grid Cybersecurity Specialist

Modern electrical grids rely heavily on IoT devices, smart meters, and remote substations, making them vulnerable to cyberattacks. These electrical engineers specialize in securing the hardware infrastructure of the power grid, implementing encrypted communications and tamper-proof hardware.

Skills required

  • SCADA systems architecture
  • Hardware encryption standards
  • Industrial Control Systems (ICS) security
  • Vulnerability penetration testing

Li-Fi Systems Developer

Li-Fi (Light Fidelity) uses modulated LED light to transmit data at extremely high speeds instead of radio waves. These engineers design the optical transceivers and modulation circuits that allow standard room lighting to function as a secure, high-bandwidth wireless network.

Skills required

  • Optoelectronics
  • Visible Light Communication (VLC) protocols
  • Photodiode and LED circuit design
  • Signal modulation (OFDM)

Direct-to-Cell Satellite Engineer

A massive leap in telecommunications occurred when satellites began connecting directly to unmodified smartphones. These engineers design massive, highly sensitive phased-array antennas for low Earth orbit satellites capable of picking up the faint radio signals of a standard cell phone from space.

Skills required

  • Phased array antenna design
  • Space-grade electronics hardening
  • Doppler shift compensation algorithms
  • Link budget calculations

Haptic Hardware Engineer

Critical for spatial computing, AR/VR, and remote robotics, haptic engineers design the electronic actuators and sensory feedback systems that simulate the feeling of touch, weight, and texture through wearable gloves, controllers, and localized surface vibrations.

Skills required

  • Electromechanical actuator design
  • Piezoelectric materials
  • Low-latency embedded processing
  • Human-computer interaction (HCI)

Flexible Electronics Architect

They develop circuits that can bend, stretch, and fold without breaking. This role is essential for the creation of foldable smartphones, smart clothing, and electronic skin patches for continuous medical monitoring.

Skills required

  • Polymer-based circuit substrates
  • Conductive ink printing technologies
  • Strain and fatigue testing
  • Miniaturized component mounting

Energy Harvesting IoT Engineer

To eliminate the need for millions of batteries in IoT devices, these engineers design circuits that scavenge ambient energy from the environment. They build systems powered entirely by indoor lighting, ambient radio waves, temperature differences, or physical vibrations.

Skills required

  • Ultra-low power electronics design
  • Piezoelectric and thermoelectric generators
  • Energy storage (Supercapacitors)
  • RF energy harvesting

Terahertz Communication Engineer

Looking beyond 5G, research has heavily shifted into the Terahertz frequency band for 6G communications. These engineers design the novel transceivers, waveguides, and antennas required to process signals at frequencies that behave like a hybrid of microwaves and light.

Skills required

  • High-frequency RF design
  • Terahertz spectroscopy
  • Advanced signal processing
  • Millimeter-wave engineering

Edge AI Silicon Architect

They design specific AI accelerator chips (NPUs and TPUs) designed to sit inside edge devices like security cameras, drones, and smartphones. Their goal is to maximize tera-operations per second (TOPS) while drawing less than a few watts of electrical power.

Skills required

  • Hardware-software co-design
  • Tensor processing logic
  • Power gating and clock domain crossing
  • C++ and hardware modeling languages

Micro-LED Systems Engineer

Micro-LED is replacing OLED as the premium display technology of the future. These engineers work on the complex backplane electronics, mass-transfer manufacturing processes, and pixel-level power distribution required to build ultra-bright, burn-in free displays for AR headsets and televisions.

Skills required

  • Display driver IC (DDIC) design
  • Thin-Film Transistor (TFT) arrays
  • Optoelectronics
  • High-yield semiconductor manufacturing

Electromagnetic Warfare (EW) Electronics Engineer

Driven by modernization in defense sectors (like DRDO and BEL), EW engineers design systems to control the electromagnetic spectrum. They develop highly classified hardware capable of jamming enemy communications, spoofing GPS signals, and protecting friendly assets from directed energy attacks.

Skills required

  • Electronic Countermeasures (ECM)
  • Radar systems and signal intelligence (SIGINT)
  • High-power microwave engineering
  • Strict defense compliance protocols

Data Center Power Density Engineer

The rise of Generative AI has caused the power requirements of data centers to skyrocket. These electrical engineers design high-voltage distribution networks, specialized busbars, and fail-safe power redundancy systems that can deliver unprecedented amounts of electricity to dense server racks.

Skills required

  • High-voltage electrical distribution
  • Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) architecture
  • Thermal-electrical co-simulation
  • Power usage effectiveness (PUE) optimization

V2X (Vehicle-to-Everything) Communications Engineer

Crucial for autonomous driving, V2X engineers design the telematics control units that allow cars to talk to each other, to traffic lights, and to pedestrian smartphones. They focus on minimizing latency to ensure cars can react to invisible road hazards in milliseconds.

Skills required

  • DSRC and C-V2X protocols
  • Automotive telematics architecture
  • Cryptographic message signing
  • RF antenna placement optimization

Space Solar Power Transmission Engineer

As nations explore harvesting solar energy in space and beaming it down to Earth, these engineers are designing the massive orbital solar arrays and the microwave power transmission electronics required to safely beam gigawatts of power through the atmosphere to ground rectennas.

Skills required

  • Wireless power transfer technologies
  • Space-grade photovoltaics
  • Microwave beam steering
  • Aerospace electrical systems
